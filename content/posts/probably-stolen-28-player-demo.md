On 29 October 2026, the day after his company shipped one of the year's larger indie launches, tinyBuild CEO Alex Nichiporchik explained where the game had come from:

> "Probably Stolen was initially a game under a different name with a released demo. That demo peaked at 28 concurrent players. We loved the concept and gameplay, and worked with the dev to reinvent the visuals and focus on what works design wise. This is the result."

The line travelled well. Within a week it had been retold across dozens of sites as a simple parable: they changed the art, and the game went viral. The post does say the visuals were reworked, and it does point at a hit. What sits between those two claims is less tidy: two demos, twenty months, a publisher, half a million wishlists, and a Steam page still advertising a game that never shipped under that name.

## The page that never shipped

`Deepspace Emporium` is still on Steam at app 3334540 — the game Rock Paper Shotgun identified on 4 November as the earlier, unnamed project in Nichiporchik's post. Both developer and publisher are listed as Questing Goose Studio, so the project appears to have been self-published. The page says "This game is not yet available on Steam", gives a planned release of 2026, and has no user reviews. It has been checked as recently as 9 November 2026.

Its description is worth reading next to the current `Probably Stolen` page. The old one: a former scavenger who used a loan to open a shop on the Lower Levels of a station split between wealthy elites and everyone else, trading goods of varying legality while rent climbs and factions pull at the business. The new one: a former scavenger with a storefront bought on a loan, goods with "flexible origins", rising rent, factions watching, and a choice about how illegal to become. The two descriptions outline much the same loop. That is a comparison of store copy, not evidence that the projects share code or a development history.

There is a stray sentence left in the old page's body: "This is the demo version of the game and does not include Save and Loading." The page appears to have carried over demo copy; how it was built or maintained since is not documented. Its user-defined tags include `Cyberpunk`, which complicates the claim that the original was simply non-cyberpunk. Those tags are applied by players, not the developer, so they are not a genre definition.

The demo itself went up on 13 February 2025 as app 3469870. Steam's indexed listing shows 69 reviews, all positive. As of 9 November 2026 its store URL no longer resolves to a product page. The publisher's 28 figure describes a low peak-concurrency number for that demo; it says nothing about total reach, and the 69 positive reviews suggest the people who did try it liked it. I could not re-read the peak directly from SteamDB, whose charts page returned 403 during research.

## What the post leaves out

Nichiporchik does not name the earlier game. In the same thread, a reply linked the old Steam page; Rock Paper Shotgun identified it as `Deepspace Emporium` on 4 November. That chain of attribution is worth keeping, because a lot of coverage compressed it into "tinyBuild confirmed the original name". None of these sources establishes how much of the code, design or development process carried from one project to the other. The Steam page shows only that a similarly described, unreleased game existed under a different name.

Rock Paper Shotgun also added the caveat that belongs on this whole story: "The situation is almost certainly more complicated than Nichiporchik makes it sound."

The most useful document is tinyBuild's own launch-day press release of 28 October. It says the shipping game had passed 500,000 wishlists, that its demo had been tried by over 660,000 unique players with 98% positive reviews, and that it reached nearly 40,000 concurrent players during Steam Next Fest — billed in the headline as the festival's biggest demo. It launched at $19.99 with a two-week 10% discount.

In other words, by the time the game went on sale, more than half a million people had already played the demo. Whatever happened between a 28-player peak and a six-figure launch was not a single art pass. What the record does show is publisher support and festival exposure, a demo under a new name, wishlists accumulated before release, and public playtests: when the studio announced 500,000 copies sold two days after launch, it credited surveys and playtest feedback for shaping the 1.0 build.

One caution about that table: 28 is a **peak concurrent** count from a February 2025 demo, while 660,000 is a **cumulative unique player** count for the 2026 demo. Dividing one by the other produces a dramatic number and no information. The other figures are dated snapshots of quantities that keep moving, and review counts vary with the language filter a source used. None of them are contradictions; they are measurements taken on different days.

## Why the story keeps recurring

Rock Paper Shotgun reached for the obvious precedents: Team Fortress 2 and Borderlands, both of which looked radically different before release. The pattern is old — a systems-heavy game whose first presentation hides the system. What the 2026 version added was a legible frame. The same outlet described the result as "Papers, Please, but you're searching for fake watermarks on cigarette packets": inspection, a counter, someone on the other side of it who might be lying. That reads in one screenshot, which matters a great deal on a store page.

None of this makes the visual overhaul irrelevant. It makes it one documented change among several: publisher support, a new name, a festival slot, public playtests and a wishlist base. The tempting version of this story is that 28 people were wrong and a six-figure launch proved them right. The record says the two numbers were never measuring the same thing.

The small detail I keep coming back to is the old page. Steam storefronts are durable. `Deepspace Emporium` still sits there with its 2026 release date, its zero reviews and its leftover line about save files, while a game with a very similar description shipped under another name in October. A page outliving its own product is not unusual on Steam. It is just unusually easy to see here.
