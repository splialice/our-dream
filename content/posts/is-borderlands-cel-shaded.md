Gearbox said no, but the terminology is more complicated than a simple yes-or-no answer. In April 2009, before the game shipped, Randy Pitchford explicitly rejected the cel-shaded label and described the visual direction as `Concept Art Style`. That is strong evidence of how the studio understood and named its art direction at the time. It is not, by itself, a technical audit of every rendering technique used in the finished game.

Asked directly on 9 April 2009, he answered by email: "No, Borderlands is not cel-shaded — it's a gritty and serious world after all." Cel shading is a rendering technique; `Concept Art Style` is an art direction. The confusion is easy to understand: the 2007 reveal was gritty, the 2009 release had heavy outlines.

## Four dated sources

- **9 April 2009 — the denial.** [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/gearbox-borderlands-is-not-cel-shaded-5206042) published Pitchford's email response under the headline `Gearbox: "Borderlands Is Not Cel-Shaded"`. This is the direct denial of the label.
- **14 April 2009 — the naming.** [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/gearbox-explain-cel-shaded-borderlands-5210855) transcribed a statement from the official Gearbox forum: the team could render its concept art in real time but had no name for the result — "We don't know what to call the result. You can use whatever term you like." Gearbox chose `Concept Art Style` for itself; it did not ask anyone else to stop using other terms. [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/pitchford-borderlands-will-not-be-cel-shaded) covered the same exchange that day; it is contemporaneous reporting, not the original source.
- **12 June 2009 — how the change happened.** A [Kotaku interview](https://kotaku.com/the-new-borderlands-look-that-almost-wasnt-5288292/) reported that four artists prototyped the new look because so many games were starting to look alike, and that Pitchford expected to reject it until he saw the prototype in motion. It explains why the 2009 screenshots look so different from the 2007 reveal.
- **27 October 2009 — the technical half.** [Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/borderlands?lang=en) published a post-launch technical write-up: Gearbox engineers extended UE3's rendering, adjusted lighting and shading for the new look, added techniques alongside the artists, and kept the work compatible with the UE3 codebase. It carries one direct Pitchford quote — "From a small group of artists came an initiative to render the concept art style in the game" — but it is an engine vendor's write-up, not a full development interview, and it does not list which art assets changed.

Checked 9–10 November 2026. The three Kotaku articles were read in full on 10 November 2026; the 14 April item verifies Kotaku's transcription of a Gearbox forum statement, not the forum post itself. The Unreal Engine page returned 403 from this machine, so its details were checked against the original during review. For the full paper trail, including TF2's 2007 conference paper and why these cases are documented so differently, see [TF2, Borderlands and the evidence behind an art makeover](/posts/tf2-borderlands-before-the-makeover.html/).

That is a denial of a label, not a technical audit: it does not prove the shipped game has no visual or technical resemblance to cel shading.
