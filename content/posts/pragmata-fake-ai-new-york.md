*Pragmata* has a city that looks like it was generated by AI. Taxis sink into the floor. Buses sprout out of walls. It is all wrong in exactly the way a machine's idea of New York would be wrong.

The twist is that no AI made it. Capcom's developers painstakingly built the distortions by hand to make the city feel machine-generated — and had to work carefully to get the mistakes right.

![Pragmata's fake AI New York](/img/editorial/pragmata-new-york.jpg)

*Official Pragmata screenshot. © CAPCOM Co., Ltd.*

## A city that has to feel "off"

In the game, protagonist Hugh and the android Diana move through a lunar facility taken over by a hostile AI. One section is a digital New York that the AI has generated.

Director Cho Yonghee and producer Naoto Oyama explained the idea in an interview with 4Gamer, translated by AUTOMATON. Cho said a familiar place helps players relate, but the team needed to signal that this was not the real thing:

> "When familiar locations appear, players can relate more easily. On top of that, to make it clear that this isn't the real New York, we wanted something slightly distorted."

Oyama named the details that sell the illusion:

> "It mirrors reality, but its unique appeal comes from the setting errors and how they feel out of place, such as taxis sinking into floors, or buses sprouting from walls."

Then came the line that makes the whole thing interesting:

> "Although the premise is that it [was] generated by AI, actually, our human developers painstakingly worked to incorporate mechanisms that express this AI-like uncanny feel."

![Pragmata lunar facility](/img/editorial/pragmata-02.jpg)

## Why hand-making the mistakes is hard

Cho said the distortion was difficult to calibrate. Push it too far and players assume the weirdness is a puzzle to solve or a hidden mechanic, not a sign that the world is fake. Pull it back and the city reads as ordinary New York. The team had to find the narrow band where a scene feels "off" without turning into a game of spot-the-difference.

This is the opposite of the usual generative-AI story in games. The headline most studios want is "we used AI to build this faster." Here the story is "we spent human effort making something look AI-generated on purpose." The uncanny is the art direction, not an accident.

## Why this is worth reading

The question a casual reader can follow is simple: **why would a game spend human effort imitating a computer's mistakes?** The answer is about environmental storytelling. The distortions tell players that this place does not obey the rules of the real world. A real distorted city would look like a bug; a deliberately distorted one says so without a line of dialogue.

It also lands at a specific moment. In 2026, "AI-generated" is a marketing label as much as a technique. Capcom built a level that trades on the look of AI slop while rejecting the process — a small, precise joke about what the label now means.
