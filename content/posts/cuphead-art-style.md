## Short answer

Studio MDHR describes Cuphead's visuals as traditional hand-drawn cel animation inspired by 1930s cartoons. In the developer interviews reviewed here, the team talks about rubber-hose animation and specific artistic references rather than presenting a unique, studio-coined name for the look. The official store page calls it "traditional hand drawn cel animation, watercolor backgrounds, and original jazz recordings." Cuphead's character animation is drawn on paper, not produced by a cel-shading shader on 3D models — the distinction is explained below.

## What it looks like

Rubber-hose limbs, pie-cut eyes, thick black outlines that change weight from frame to frame, watercolour backgrounds, and the dust, scratches and slight misregistration of old film. The characters move the way Fleischer and Disney shorts moved: a body can stretch, flatten or turn into a loose collection of parts before snapping back.

Those are not a single filter over a 3D scene. In a [2017 interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/how-i-cuphead-i-nailed-the-look-and-feel-of-classic-1930s-cartoons), Chad Moldenhauer said the team studied "the line weights, how the thickness of the tapers would change from frame to frame on all of the old cartoons" and "mimicked that to the best of our ability." He added that the visible noise was physical: "It's actually scanned from film, it's not digital ... the dust and the hairs and all of that noise is like, real life stuff."

## What the developers said

Neither of the two interviews reviewed here presents a unique, studio-coined name for the look. In the [2018 IGF interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-studio-mdhr-s-i-cuphead-i-), asked how the style was chosen, the team answered procedurally: "We tested out a few story-book and cartoon styles but kept coming back to the rubber-hose animation of the 30's. Everything just sort of grew from there."

The same interview names a specific reference: "The core of Cuphead's aesthetic matches closest with the Fleischer Brothers' work, especially *Swing You Sinners*, which perfectly encapsulates the mood and insanity that we wanted to put into the game." In the 2017 piece, Moldenhauer described the earliest version more loosely: "We pulled in some Mickey Mouse and some Popeye and made this kind of Frankenstein pieced-together scenes." The official store page uses the descriptive phrase "traditional hand drawn cel animation."

That boundary matters. These sources show how the studio described the technique and its references. They do not establish that no name was ever used anywhere; that would require a wider survey than this one.

## How it was made

The 2018 interview is direct: "All of the art is hand drawn and hand inked the old fashioned way, and then colored in Photoshop." So the drawings and ink work were analogue; the colour stage was digital.

The [2017 article](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/how-i-cuphead-i-nailed-the-look-and-feel-of-classic-1930s-cartoons) adds why. Everything was first drawn on paper, then inked on paper and coloured digitally rather than inked and painted on a cel as tradition would have it. The reporter notes that the team tried a cel test and found it made little difference in the game; Moldenhauer's own words are that the shortcut "saved us a ton of time, and was probably a major contributor to why we were eventually able to finish the game." The studio also recreated old typefaces and designed custom ones based on 1930s comics and film title cards.

One detail separates this from most surface-level descriptions. The art did not drive the level design. "Pretty much all of the boss battles were created before any art was applied to them," the developers said. "We weren't creating boss battles to fit the animation, we were creating animation to fit the boss battles." Once the gameplay was in, the characters went through revisions with the animators to make sure "they fit with the in-place hit boxes and attack timing", so the strange movements had to stay readable as gameplay rather than becoming pure cartoon gesture.

The visual language had rules too. "We could basically do anything we wanted, as long as the rubber hose limbs and pie eyes were there, it would fit," they said — but found that "taking the crazy up a notch helped the most." Escalation was deliberate: fights were designed to start strange and keep growing.

## Common confusions

**Cel animation and cel shading.** Cuphead's signature character animation was made from hand-drawn 2D artwork, not by applying a conventional cel-shading shader to 3D character models. Cel shading and traditional cel animation share a historical term but describe different production methods. This is a claim about the main method used to make the characters move; it does not assert that the game engine contains no shaders at all. Our [What Is Cel Shading?](/posts/what-is-cel-shading.html/) guide sets out the technical distinction.

**A filter versus a production method.** The period look was reconstructed element by element — changing line weight, scanned film dust, custom lettering, watercolour backgrounds. A post-process filter can imitate some of the surface; it cannot explain the production order described above. This is an editorial comparison, not a community claim documented in the sources.

**Fleischer as the only influence.** *Swing You Sinners* is named as the closest match, but Mickey Mouse and Popeye also appear in the developers' account of the early experiments. Treating one studio as the sole influence goes beyond the record.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the name of the Cuphead art style?**  
The source-backed answer is **traditional hand-drawn cel animation**. Studio MDHR's store page uses that phrase and adds watercolour backgrounds and original jazz recordings; the developer interviews reviewed here describe rubber-hose animation and 1930s cartoon references. They do not present a unique, studio-coined style name, so "traditional hand-drawn cel animation" is the clearest answer — not "cel shading."

**Is Cuphead cel shaded?**  
Not for the character animation: it was drawn and inked on paper, then coloured digitally. Cel shading normally means quantising lighting on a 3D model. The two terms share "cel" but describe different production methods.
