Gearbox called it `Concept Art Style`. That was the studio's own name for Borderlands' art direction, settled on in April 2009, a few months before release. The term widely used by media and players — cel-shaded — is one its CEO explicitly rejected that same month. Both statements are on the record, which is why the question keeps coming back.

## The name on the record

On 9 April 2009, [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/gearbox-borderlands-is-not-cel-shaded-5206042) published Randy Pitchford's email reply under the headline `Gearbox: "Borderlands Is Not Cel-Shaded"`:

> "No, Borderlands is not cel-shaded — it's a gritty and serious world after all."

Five days later, [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/gearbox-explain-cel-shaded-borderlands-5210855) transcribed a longer statement from the official Gearbox forum, in which Pitchford said that since the game was first unveiled the team had "made big advancements with the art direction and the technology to support the art, and have produced some pretty impressive, even shocking results." New techniques let the game render its concept art in real time, but there was no agreed name for the result: "We don't know what to call the result. You can use whatever term you like." Gearbox settled on `Concept Art Style` for itself. It did not ask anyone else to stop saying cel-shaded. [Eurogamer](https://www.eurogamer.net/pitchford-borderlands-will-not-be-cel-shaded) covered the same exchange that day; it is contemporaneous reporting, not the original source.

One boundary worth keeping: the 14 April wording comes from Kotaku's transcription of a Gearbox forum statement. The original forum page has not been independently checked.

## Why the other label stuck

Two things make the confusion durable. First, the game did not look like this at the start. Borderlands was unveiled in 2007 as "Diablo meets Mad Max", with a gritty realistic look. By the 2009 previews it had heavy ink outlines and a hand-painted feel. A visible change invites a name, and cel-shaded was the nearest one to hand.

Second, the two terms are not the same kind of thing. Cel shading is a rendering technique. `Concept Art Style` is an art direction — a decision about what the game should look like, not a claim about which shader produced it. Calling the game cel-shaded is a visual classification; `Concept Art Style` is a label its maker chose. One can be a reasonable description while the other remains the official name. That is also why a denial of the label is not a technical audit of the shipped renderer.

## Where the look came from

A [Kotaku interview](https://kotaku.com/the-new-borderlands-look-that-almost-wasnt-5288292/) of 12 June 2009 supplies the origin. Four artists prototyped the new direction after growing frustrated that so many games looked alike — "So four of them got together and prototyped this new look out." Pitchford expected to reject it, changed his mind after seeing the prototype running in real time, and the proposal then won support from Gearbox management and publisher 2K.

A post-launch [Unreal Engine article](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/borderlands?lang=en) of 27 October 2009 covers the technical side: Gearbox engineers extended UE3's rendering capabilities, adjusted lighting and shading for the new look, and worked with artists to implement additional rendering techniques while maintaining compatibility with the UE3 codebase. It carries one direct Pitchford quote — "From a small group of artists came an initiative to render the concept art style in the game" — but it is an engine vendor's write-up rather than a full development interview, and it does not list which assets changed. ## So which term should you use?

If you are quoting the studio, use `Concept Art Style` and attribute it to April 2009. If you are describing what the game looks like, cel-shaded is a defensible shorthand — with the caveat that Gearbox disputed it at the time. What does not survive contact with the record is treating the two as synonyms, or saying the studio never addressed the question.
