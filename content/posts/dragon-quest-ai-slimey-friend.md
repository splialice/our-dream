Dragon Quest creator Yuji Horii doesn't want to replace every townsperson with AI. He wants something stranger: a Slimey companion players can ask for advice — and perhaps tell their problems to.

Speaking at Google Cloud Next 2026, Horii described the idea behind "Oshaberi Slimey" (Chatty Slimey), a Gemini-powered companion Square Enix and Google are building for *Dragon Quest X*: an AI character that players might come to think of as a friend.

## "Not just a tool, but a friend"

Square Enix and Google announced the project in March 2026, with a closed beta recruitment campaign beginning on March 21. Horii's remarks in Las Vegas in May gave the clearest reason for it yet. Toyo Keizai reported that he sees AI as "not just a convenient tool, but a friend to each individual player." AUTOMATON's English coverage quotes him going further:

> "When I first created Dragon Quest, I wanted the townspeople dialogue to sound as much like real human speech as possible. Now, AI can actually respond, so I think we can make them feel even more human. However, simply replacing townspeople with AI wouldn't be fun."

That last sentence is the design choice. Horii is not arguing for AI to replace every NPC in the world. He points to a smaller, stranger use: a side companion you talk to, like an assistant, but wrapped in a game character you already like.

## Why a game character lowers the barrier

Horii's most interesting point is about how people feel about talking to AI. Ask someone to confide in a chatbot and many will hesitate. Put the same chatbot inside a slime:

> "With regular AI, people can feel embarrassed about treating it like a friend, but if it's a game character, the barrier is lower, and people may find it easier to talk to about various topics."

He imagines the companion doing two jobs at once — helping with the game and listening to things that have nothing to do with it:

> "I think it's wonderful that [Slimey] can also listen to people's concerns with empathy and even talk about things unrelated to the game itself... AI characters could become an entry point that teaches beginners how to play, and even after leaving the game, players might come to consider them as friends."

In practice, Chatty Slimey will read game progress, screenshots and inventory to advise on what to do next, and support real-time voice through Gemini Live. A beta has run; the release date is still unannounced.

## The beta revealed a bigger problem

The idea sounds charming, but making it work reliably is another matter. In an August 2026 update, Square Enix said the beta surfaced numerous issues that had not been visible before. Players enjoyed talking to the AI more than the team expected, but before a public launch the studio still needed to improve the accuracy of in-game information and conversation, and address safety concerns. Official service remained unscheduled.

That is the unglamorous half of the story: the hard part is not the idea of an AI friend, but keeping its answers correct and its behaviour safe inside a live game.

## Early reactions reveal three very different concerns

This is a small comment section, not a market survey — the AUTOMATON article drew seven comments. Within that small sample, one reader wrote "noone wants this shit in games," while another said it was "actually something people want to see AI used for," praising a "baby steps" approach. A third worried about getting too attached: after an adventure, would you feel obliged to keep playing just to keep your "friends" alive?

That split — rejection, cautious approval, and attachment anxiety — is worth noting as a reaction, not as evidence of what Dragon Quest players as a whole think. Horii's argument is not that players will definitely accept an AI friend. It is that a game character is the least awkward container yet invented for one.

## Why this is worth reading

The question is small and specific: **why make an AI slime you can befriend?** Horii's approach is narrower than replacing an entire town with AI. He is testing whether a familiar game character can make conversational AI feel more approachable. Whether that companion can become a "friend" after the game ends is his bet, not a proven result.
