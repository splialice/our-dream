## Two scores, two different games

GTA 6 launched on 19 November 2026. Take-Two's [official release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/946581/000162828026075217/exhibit9918k.htm) put day-one retail sales at $3.79 billion — an estimate of retail sales through the end of 19 November worldwide, not the company's net revenue. It was, by that measure, the biggest entertainment launch on record.

Then the other number arrived. On Metacritic's PS5 page, critics scored it 99/100. Players pulled their score toward 5.6/10. Those are different scales — 5.6/10 is 56/100, so the gap is about 43 points, not 93 — but the direction is unmistakable. User scores kept moving through the week: a 23 November report logged 98,623 ratings, and by 24 November the count was over 110,000. Any figure quoted here is a snapshot, not a settled number.

The argument underneath those scores is worth taking seriously, because it is not really about graphics or bugs. It is about what people thought GTA was for.

## One of the loudest complaints is about Lucia

A visible strand of the backlash is directed at Lucia Caminos, the game's first playable female lead. It is a real reaction, but it is not a universal one — and it is not measurable from the threads that get shared the most. What can be said is that she is one of the most discussed characters in the launch-week argument.

Asmongold's version: "impulsive, stupid, jealous, she doesn't understand her place in the relationship, she's not skilled, she's selfish, is emotionally manipulative." A player quoted in [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/gta-6-players-debate-domestic-activities-criminal-gameplay-1810158) objected to specific beats — leaving after an argument, having the child alone, leaving the kid in a café while looking for money, getting out of prison focused on getting rich, wearing an ankle monitor and still running around. A C_Chat thread collected the same complaint from players across regions, and it is fair to say the reaction travelled: but "everyone, everywhere" would be a claim no set of forum threads can support.

There is a counter-reading too. One player who had put 13 hours in argued that the scenes critics call "soft Jason" are ordinary couple arguments before either character knew about the child, and that readers looking for constant set pieces were never the audience for this sort of open-world story. That post was smaller than the complaints. Worth reading anyway, because it changes what kind of argument this is: not "is Lucia badly written" but "is a difficult protagonist the same thing as a bad one."

Rockstar has form here. GTA has always written characters who grate — Catalina, Dutch, the whole tradition of people who make spectacularly bad decisions. The difference is that those characters eventually became villains or got written out. Lucia is a co-lead you are stuck with for a hundred hours, and that is a different proposition.

## What the game actually does, what players say it does, and what people think it does

This is where the argument gets slippery, so it is worth separating three things.

**What is in the game.** [AFP's 25 November report](https://www.afp.com/en/gta-gone-soft-fans-fume-over-games-romantic-streak) describes a new honour system that rewards good deeds and discourages wanton violence: helping strangers, not littering, staying in shape. Romance is a system with feedback rather than a scripted switch. [Rolling Stone's 19 November piece](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-news/gta-vi-girlfriend-wife-nice-gamers-chuds-1235661480) is careful on this point: you are not required to be thoughtful toward Lucia, but being a jerk makes her like you less. Street prostitution, present since *GTA III* in 2001, is absent. Strip clubs remain, and Jason and Lucia have intimate scenes tied to relationship level.

**What players say about it.** Asmongold complained about carrying Lucia's clothes and groceries from the trunk to the apartment. Stephen Crowder: "you can build your girl IKEA furniture, do her nails, buy her flowers, but you can't pick up hookers anymore." On X, "cuck simulator" and "simp simulator" became the shorthand. One streamer spent 45 minutes on painting and roof repairs; whether that particular clip represents the typical experience is a judgement call, but the reaction to chores in a crime game was broad enough that Rockstar addressed it.

**What got repeated without evidence.** A numbered checklist circulating on Chinese forums asked whether cars could no longer be stolen and whether the writing team was all-female — these were questions, not confirmed features. The "all-female writers" image was a photo from Rockstar's [International Women's Day post](https://www.techroomage.com/blog/gta-polarized-reviews-overseas-rumors-analysis) on 8 March, recycled in November as proof of a direction. Claims about $100 ultimate-edition buyers refunding after three hours spread widely; the trend was discussed, but a specific sample was not established in the reporting reviewed here. That does not make the frustration imaginary — when a franchise sells you on chaos and delivers a relationship-management sim, people will believe the worst version of every rumour. That is arguably the more damning fact.

## Rockstar's answer

Rockstar broke its usual silence on the prostitution question. As reported by Shacknews and relayed in several outlets, the studio said GTA 6 tells the story of "a couple in desperate trouble, on the run," and that every system carried into the game was chosen to serve the story's mood and the systems being deepened — wanted levels, radio stations, robberies, car theft. Older elements that did not fit were left out. The removal was about focus on Jason and Lucia, not about becoming "woke"; adult content still exists through the strip club and the couple's own intimate scenes.

AFP's report frames the wider design the same way: a modern Bonnie-and-Clyde pair running for their future and their child, where players must balance the relationship and their criminal profile to reach a better ending. That is a coherent design position. It also explains why the reception split so cleanly: the systems are not bugs, they are the point — for one audience.

## The idea underneath the anger

Here is the part worth keeping after the memes fade. GTA was the place you went to be the person you could not be in real life. Not a good person. Not a considerate partner who remembers the flowers. The appeal was that the world kept no moral ledger, that you could be awful without the game quietly tutoring you.

GTA 6 adds a ledger: a relationship that has opinions about you, an honour system that rewards decency, domestic tasks that sit between you and the next robbery. Critics saw a mature, character-driven follow-up with real stakes. Many players saw the last playground where being decent was optional, with the option quietly narrowed.

Those two readings can both be honest. The 99 is for a game. The 5.6 is for GTA.

## What happens next

None of this is settled. Rockstar's track record is one of shipping fixes and letting the conversation cool: GTA Online was a mess for years and got rebuilt. Metacritic user scores move fast in launch week, and the 23 and 24 November rating counts already show the sample growing. In a month this may read differently.

The design case is also real. A Bonnie-and-Clyde story where the relationship has weight is a more interesting idea than another hundred hours of aimless chaos. Because Lucia is a co-lead rather than a side character, the writing has to give her a life outside Jason, including the parts that make people uncomfortable. An easy-to-like protagonist is an easy-to-forget one.

But that is a discussion about craft. The launch-week anger was about something else: thirteen years of waiting, and the discovery that the game which used to let you be bad now wants you to be better. Whether that is a betrayal or a maturation is exactly the argument worth having — and it is a better argument than whether Lucia is annoying.
