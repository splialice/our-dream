## Short answer

A **visual novel** is a narrative-focused game form: the player advances text with character art and sound, and may encounter choices. A **dating sim** is a simulation subgenre about romance, often with a calendar, stats or a "love meter." They overlap because many romance visual novels use dating-sim mechanics, but the labels describe different things: story presentation versus relationship simulation.

## What each term points to

Academic research supports a cautious definition. Kumiko Saito's 2021 *Arts* article calls visual novels "intensely narrative-oriented games in a novel-like format" and says their interaction is often limited to "pushing a button to turn pages and read." By contrast, she writes that "Gaming components were vital to dating simulation games in which the player controls the male avatar and uses numerical parameters to measure romantic relationships." Camingue, Carstensdottir and Melcer's 2021 ACM paper built a definition from "30 prior academic definitions and 54 existing VNs" yet still observed that "there is not a singular definition" of the genre.

Wikipedia's visual novel article defines the genre as "a video game genre of Japanese origin focused on presenting a story," with progress made through "clicking, tapping or pressing a button." It also notes that academic definitions vary. That is the honest starting point: visual novel is a broad, disputed label.

The same article says the English expression was applied retroactively to many games: "The expression 'visual novel' emerged in the mid-1990s and has been applied retroactively in the Western world to a vast corpus of games." In Japan, by contrast, visual novels are treated as a subgenre of adventure games. So the word you search in English does not map perfectly to Japanese genre labels.

A dating sim is narrower. Wikipedia defines dating sims as "a video game subgenre of simulation games with romantic elements." Unlike a purely narrative visual novel, a dating sim often adds "an additional statistical and time management layer," such as a calendar, scheduled dates, stat raising or a character attraction meter.

Ren'Py's own site gives a useful technical boundary from the tool side. It describes Ren'Py as "a visual novel engine" that helps creators "use words, images, and sounds to tell interactive stories," and says those stories "can be both visual novels and life simulation games." In other words, the same engine and presentation style can support both labels.

## Why people mix them up

The overlap is real. A romance visual novel may ask the player to choose dialogue, pursue one character's route, and unlock a romantic ending. A dating sim may present those choices through dialogue boxes and character sprites. From the outside they can look identical.

The difference is what the system is doing underneath. In the visual novel description above, choices branch the narrative. In the dating sim description, choices may change a love meter, consume in-game time, or raise stats that determine later scenes. One label emphasizes story structure; the other emphasizes relationship simulation.

This is why broad English searches are messy. Western players may call a narrative romance game a dating sim because it has romance, while Japanese categories and modern English usage may call the same game a visual novel because of how it is presented. The Wikipedia article even records the reverse problem: games such as *Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney* may be called visual novels by Western audiences although Japanese players would not necessarily categorize them that way.

## A practical rule

| If the main activity is... | The closer label is... |
| :--- | :--- |
| Reading text, advancing scenes, choosing story branches | Visual novel |
| Managing time, stats or affection to build a relationship | Dating sim |
| Both reading branches and managing romance systems | Both labels can apply |

This rule is descriptive, not a genre law. Some games deliberately blur the boundary, and some developers use different terms in marketing. But it is more useful than treating the two words as synonyms.

## Related terms, not a family tree

If you came from `visual novel history` or `galgame meaning`, the short version is: English-language audiences often use **visual novel** as a wider umbrella for story-heavy Japanese games. The nearby labels are not a strict hierarchy; each emphasizes a different dimension.

| Term | Main dimension it describes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Visual Novel | Narrative presentation and interaction form |
| Dating Sim | Romance relationship simulation mechanics |
| Bishoujo Game | A bishoujo-centred game tradition and audience positioning |
| Otome Game | A female-oriented romance game tradition |
| Eroge | An adult-content attribute |

A single game can carry several of these labels at once. That is why the useful question is not “which box does it belong in?” but “which dimension does this term describe?”
